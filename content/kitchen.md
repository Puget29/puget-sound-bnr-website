## Planning Your Kitchen Remodel: Layout and Budget Basics

The decisions you make before construction starts have the biggest effect on how the kitchen feels and what it costs. Here is how we think about them with every homeowner.

### Start with how you use the kitchen

Before choosing finishes, think about how the room really works. Who cooks, and how often? Do you entertain? Do kids do homework at the counter? Is there a second cook? The answers decide whether you need an island, a bigger pantry, more counter space beside the stove, or just better storage and lighting.

### Layout choices

- **Keeping the existing layout** is the most budget friendly path. Sinks, gas lines and appliances stay where they are, so plumbing and electrical work stays simple.
- **Improving the layout** within the same footprint, for example by changing the cabinet plan or adding an island, can transform how the kitchen works without moving walls.
- **Opening the kitchen** to the dining or living room, or moving the sink or range, creates the biggest change. It also adds structural, plumbing and permit work, so it should be planned early.

### How a kitchen budget usually breaks down

Every project differs, but as general industry guidance, the money in a kitchen remodel usually goes roughly like this:

| Where the money goes | Typical share of the budget |
| --- | --- |
| Cabinets and hardware | 25% to 35% |
| Labor and installation | 15% to 20% |
| Appliances and ventilation | 10% to 15% |
| Countertops | About 10% |
| Flooring, lighting, plumbing fixtures, paint and tile | The remaining share |

These are planning guides, not a quote. Your real numbers depend on your home, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve. Many Seattle area homes are decades old, and walls sometimes reveal things nobody could see before, such as outdated wiring, hidden water damage or old materials that need special handling. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Permits

Many kitchen changes need a permit, especially moving walls, relocating plumbing or gas lines, or changing electrical. Simple updates, like replacing cabinets and countertops in the same place, often do not. Rules differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, so we check what applies to your project during the planning stage.

## Cabinets, Countertops and Where Quality Actually Matters

You touch your cabinets and counters every day, so this is where quality shows up. Here is how we help homeowners choose.

### Cabinets

Cabinets are the biggest single cost and the part of the kitchen you live with longest. The main options are:

- **Stock cabinets.** Ready-made sizes, the lowest cost and the fastest to get. Good for simple layouts and tight budgets.
- **Semi-custom cabinets.** More sizes, finishes and storage options, with a good balance of cost and flexibility.
- **Custom cabinets.** Built to your exact space and needs. They make the most of unusual rooms and give you the widest choice of design, at the highest cost.

What matters most is not the door style but the construction: solid wood or plywood boxes instead of thin particleboard, strong joints, drawers on full-extension soft-close slides, and a finish that holds up to moisture and daily use. Good installation matters just as much. Cabinets that are not level and tight to the wall never look right, no matter how nice they are.

### Countertops

- **Quartz** is durable, resists stains and needs very little care, which is why it is so popular.
- **Granite and natural stone** bring a one of a kind look. Most stone needs periodic sealing.
- **Butcher block** is warm and can be refinished, but it needs regular care around water.
- **Solid surface and laminate** can work well when the budget is tight.

We will show you samples in your own kitchen light, because stone and color look different there than in a showroom.

### Details that are easy to overlook

Backsplash, lighting and ventilation make a kitchen feel finished. Under-cabinet lighting makes counters easier to work on. A properly sized range hood that vents outside keeps cooking smoke and moisture out of the house. Outlets in the right places save you from extension cords later. We plan these early, because they are much harder to fix after the cabinets are in.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on the things you touch every day and cannot easily replace: cabinet quality, drawer hardware, countertops and good installation. Save on things that are easy to upgrade later, such as decorative lighting fixtures, paint colors and some hardware. Our job is to help you put the budget where you will notice it.

## Living Through a Kitchen Renovation

A remodel disrupts daily life for a while, and it is better to know that up front. Here is what to expect and how we make it easier.

- **Timeline.** Planning and design usually take a few weeks. Cabinets are often the longest wait, commonly several weeks to a few months depending on the type. Construction itself typically takes a few weeks for a smaller remodel and longer for a full renovation. We give you a specific schedule in writing once the plan is set.
- **No kitchen for a while.** Set up a temporary kitchen before demolition: a microwave, a coffee maker, a small cooler or fridge and a table in another room. Plan simple meals, and keep a list of favorite takeout.
- **Dust and noise.** Demolition is the loudest and dirtiest stage. We seal off the work area, protect floors and surfaces, and clean up at the end of every work day.
- **Pets and children.** Keep them away from the work zone. We will tell you when the noisiest days are so you can plan around them.
- **Decisions.** We will ask you to choose finishes, fixtures and colors on a schedule, because late changes slow the project and add cost. We help you make each choice in time.
- **Communication.** You will know who is on site, what is happening this week and what comes next. If something unexpected appears behind a wall, we explain it, show you the options and agree on the next step with you before we continue.

A well planned remodel feels calm even when the kitchen is torn apart, and that is what we aim for.

## Kitchen Remodeling FAQs

### How long does a kitchen remodel take?

It depends on the scope. A refresh with new cabinets and countertops in the same layout takes less time than a full renovation with layout changes. Most of the schedule is made up of design, permits where needed, and cabinet lead times, with construction after that. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does a kitchen remodel cost?

The cost depends on the size of the kitchen, the layout, the cabinets and countertops you choose, the appliances, and how much plumbing and electrical work is needed. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your kitchen and give you a clear written estimate.

### Do I need a permit to remodel my kitchen?

Often yes, especially when the work involves moving walls, plumbing, gas lines or electrical. Replacing cabinets and countertops in the same locations may not need one. Requirements differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, and we help you with the permit requirements for your project.

### Can I live in my home during the remodel?

Most homeowners do. You will be without a working kitchen for part of the project, so we suggest setting up a temporary kitchen in another room. We contain dust, protect your floors and clean up at the end of each day.

### Can you help me design the layout?

Yes. Planning and design are part of the process. We look at how you use the kitchen, suggest layout options and help you choose cabinets, countertops, lighting and finishes that work together.

### Should I keep my existing layout to save money?

Often that is a smart choice. Keeping sinks, gas lines and appliances where they are keeps plumbing and electrical work simple. We will tell you honestly whether a layout change is worth it for the way you live, or whether a better cabinet plan in the same footprint gives you most of the benefit.

### Why do cabinets take so long to arrive?

Cabinets are made to order, and the lead time depends on the type. Stock cabinets are the fastest, and semi-custom and custom cabinets take longer. We place cabinet orders as early as the plan allows so construction does not stop while you wait.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your home, look at the kitchen, and talk about your goals, your wish list and your budget. You do not need a finished design. Bring photos or ideas if you have them. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
