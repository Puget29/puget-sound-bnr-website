## Planning Your Painting Project: Prep, Products and Budget Basics

The decisions you make before the first coat have the biggest effect on how the paint looks and how long it lasts. Here is how we think about them with every homeowner.

### Start with the surfaces

Before choosing colors, think about the condition of what is being painted. Is the paint peeling, chalking or stained? Are there cracks, holes or soft spots? Has a wall been patched before? The answers decide how much preparation is needed, and preparation is often more of the job than the painting itself.

### How long paint typically lasts

Every home differs, and the lifespan of a paint job depends on the surface, the preparation, the products and how much weather and wear it takes. As general industry guidance, typical ranges look like this:

| Surface | Typical time between repaints |
| --- | --- |
| Exterior siding | About 7 to 15 years |
| Exterior trim and doors | About 5 to 10 years |
| Interior walls | About 5 to 10 years |
| Kitchens and bathrooms | About 5 to 8 years |
| Interior ceilings | 10 years or more |

These are planning guides, not a promise about your home. Your real numbers depend on your house, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### How a painting budget usually breaks down

As general industry guidance, the money in a painting project usually goes roughly like this:

| Where the money goes | Typical share of the budget |
| --- | --- |
| Labor, including preparation | 55% to 70% |
| Paint and primer | 15% to 25% |
| Repairs, caulking and supplies | The remaining share |

These are planning guides, not a quote. Notice that most of the money goes into the labor and preparation, not the paint itself. That is a good sign, because that is where lasting results come from.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve, especially on exteriors. Once old paint comes off, we sometimes find rot, damaged trim or surfaces that need more repair than anyone could see from the ground. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Older homes and lead paint

Homes built before 1978 may contain lead based paint, which calls for special handling during preparation. If your home is older, we raise this early and plan for it as part of the scope.

## Preparation, Products and Where Quality Actually Matters

Great paint jobs are made of small, careful steps that happen before the color goes on. Here is how we help homeowners decide where to put the budget.

### Preparation

Paint sticks to clean, sound, properly prepared surfaces. That means washing off dirt and mildew, scraping loose paint, sanding rough areas, filling holes and cracks, and spot priming bare or stained spots. Skipping these steps is the most common reason paint fails early.

### Exterior painting in the Pacific Northwest

Exterior paint in Puget Sound deals with rain, damp air, moss and mildew for much of the year. Surfaces need to be dry before painting, and the weather decides when exterior work can happen, usually in drier months. We plan around the forecast, seal gaps and edges where water enters, and use products made for wet climates.

### Interior painting details

On the inside, what you notice is the finish: smooth walls, sharp lines at the ceiling and trim, no drips or roller marks, and consistent color from corner to corner. We protect floors and furniture, cut clean edges and apply enough coats for solid, even coverage.

### Paint quality and sheen

Better paint covers more evenly, resists wear and holds its color longer. Sheen matters too. Flat and matte finishes hide imperfections on walls and ceilings. Satin and semi gloss finishes stand up better to cleaning in kitchens and bathrooms and on trim. We help you choose the right product for each surface.

### Choosing colors

Color looks different in every home, depending on the light, the windows and the time of day. We help you test colors in your own light before committing, so you are not surprised by how a color looks on the whole wall.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on preparation, good primer and quality paint on the surfaces that take the most weather and wear, such as the exterior and high traffic areas. Save on things that are easy to change later, such as accent walls and secondary rooms. Our job is to help you put the budget where it lasts.

## Living Through a Painting Project

A painting project disrupts your home for a short time, and it is better to know that up front. Here is what to expect and how we make it easier.

- **Timeline.** Many interior projects take from a few days to a couple of weeks, and exterior projects can take longer depending on the size of the house and how much preparation is needed. Weather can move exterior schedules, and we tell you plainly if it does. We give you a specific schedule in writing once the plan is set.
- **Furniture and belongings.** We move and cover furniture where we can, and we ask you to clear small items, wall art and valuables from the work areas.
- **Smell and ventilation.** Fresh paint has a smell, and we choose products and plan ventilation to keep it manageable. We tell you which rooms to avoid and for how long.
- **Drying time.** Paint needs time to dry and cure before it takes wear. We tell you when it is safe to put furniture back and clean the surfaces.
- **Pets and children.** Keep them away from the work zone and from wet paint. We will tell you when the work is happening in each area, so you can plan around it.
- **Communication.** You will know who is on site, what is happening this week and what comes next. If something unexpected appears when we start, we explain it, show you the options and agree on the next step with you before we continue.

A well planned painting project feels organized even when every room has drop cloths, and that is what we aim for.

## Painting FAQs

### How long does a painting project take?

It depends on the size of the area, the condition of the surfaces and how much preparation is needed. A single room takes much less time than a whole house, and an exterior depends on the weather. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does painting cost?

The cost depends on the size of the area, the condition of the surfaces, the amount of preparation and repair, and the products you choose. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your home and give you a clear written estimate.

### What is the best time of year to paint the exterior?

Exterior painting works best in dry weather, when surfaces are dry and temperatures are moderate. In the Puget Sound area that is usually in the drier months. We plan around the forecast and tell you plainly if weather changes the schedule.

### Do you move furniture and protect my belongings?

We move and cover furniture where we can, and we protect floors and surfaces. We ask you to clear small items, wall art and valuables from the work areas before we start.

### How many coats of paint will I need?

It depends on the surface and the color change. Most surfaces get primer where needed, followed by two finish coats for even coverage and a lasting result. We tell you what each area needs in your written scope.

### My home was built before 1978. Is that a problem?

Older homes may contain lead based paint, which calls for special handling during preparation. If your home is older, we raise this early and plan for it as part of the scope.

### Can you help me choose colors?

Yes. We help you choose colors and sheens, and we suggest testing them in your own light before the full job, since color looks different in every home.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your home, look at the surfaces, and talk about what you want painted, the colors you have in mind, your goals and your budget. You do not need to have everything decided. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
