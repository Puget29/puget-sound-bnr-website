## Planning Your Siding Project: Repair, Replace and Budget Basics

The decision between repairing and replacing siding has the biggest effect on what you spend. Here is how we think about it with every homeowner.

### Signs your siding needs attention

- Boards that are warped, cracked, split or loose
- Soft, spongy or rotted wood, especially near the bottom of walls and around windows
- Paint that is peeling, bubbling or flaking again soon after being repainted
- Stains, mold or mildew that return after cleaning
- Gaps, holes or damage where water can get in
- Interior walls with stains, bubbling paint or a musty smell near exterior walls
- Siding that is simply old and worn, even if there are no obvious problems yet

### Repair or replace

- **A repair** makes sense when the damage is limited and the rest of the siding is in good shape. A few damaged boards or a failed seal around one window can often be fixed without replacing everything.
- **A replacement** makes sense when the siding is near the end of its life, when water damage is widespread or when problems keep coming back. Paying for repeated repairs on worn out siding usually costs more than replacing it.
- **We will tell you honestly which one you need.** Sometimes that means a smaller job than you expected, and that is fine with us.

### Siding materials and how long they typically last

Every house differs, and lifespan depends on installation, moisture protection and upkeep. As general industry guidance, typical ranges look like this:

| Material | Typical lifespan |
| --- | --- |
| Vinyl siding | About 20 to 40 years |
| Fiber cement siding | About 30 to 50 years |
| Wood siding | About 20 to 40 years with upkeep |
| Engineered wood siding | About 20 to 30 years |
| Metal siding | About 40 to 70 years |

These are planning guides, not a promise about your home. Your real numbers depend on your house, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### How a siding budget usually breaks down

As general industry guidance, the money in a siding project usually goes roughly like this:

| Where the money goes | Typical share of the budget |
| --- | --- |
| Labor and installation | 35% to 45% |
| Siding materials | 30% to 40% |
| Trim, flashing and moisture protection | 10% to 15% |
| Paint, finishing, removal and cleanup | The remaining share |

These are planning guides, not a quote.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve for a replacement. Once old siding comes off, we sometimes find damaged sheathing, old leaks, rot or outdated materials that nobody could see from outside. Many Seattle area homes are decades old, and some older materials need special handling. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Permits

Whether you need a permit depends on the scope. Replacing siding in the same place often needs less review than work that changes the structure, windows, doors or sheathing. Rules differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, so we check what applies to your project during the planning stage.

## Siding in the Pacific Northwest: Where Quality Actually Matters

Siding in a dry climate is not the same as siding in Puget Sound. The most important work on a siding project is often the work that disappears behind the boards. Here is what we plan for.

### Keeping water out

Siding is the first line of defense, not the last. Behind it, a weather resistant barrier and careful flashing handle the water that always finds its way past the surface. When these layers are skipped or installed badly, water collects behind the boards and rots the wall from the inside, long before anything shows on the outside.

### Windows, doors and penetrations

Most leaks start at openings: windows, doors, vents, lights, hose bibs and corners. Each one needs proper flashing and sealing in the right order. We spend our time on these details because they decide how long the wall stays dry.

### Airflow behind the siding

Walls that can dry out last longer. Depending on the siding and the house, we look at ways to give moisture somewhere to go, so the wall does not stay damp through the wet months.

### Gaps, clearances and trim

Siding needs the right clearance above the ground, decks, roofs and hard surfaces, so splashing water and standing water do not wick up into the boards. We plan these clearances, and the trim that finishes them, as part of the job.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on what you cannot easily redo: the weather barrier, flashing, quality fasteners and careful installation. A good installation of simple siding is worth more than a poor installation of expensive siding. Save on things that are easy to change later, such as paint color and decorative trim. Our job is to help you put the budget where it protects your home.

## Siding FAQs

### How long does siding replacement take?

It depends on the size of the house, the material and how much damage we find. Many projects take from a few days to a few weeks, and weather can move the schedule. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does siding replacement cost?

The cost depends on the size of the house, the material you choose, how much repair is needed behind the old siding and how complex the walls are. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your home and give you a clear written estimate.

### Should I repair or replace my siding?

If the damage is limited and the rest of the siding is in good shape, a repair is often enough. If the siding is near the end of its life, or water damage is widespread, replacement usually makes more sense. We will tell you honestly which one fits your home.

### How do I know if there is water damage behind my siding?

Common signs are soft or spongy boards, peeling paint that keeps coming back, stains, mold or mildew, and a musty smell or stains on the inside of exterior walls. Water often travels before it shows up, so damage can be larger than it looks. An inspection finds the source.

### Do I need a permit to replace my siding?

It depends on the scope, especially if the work involves sheathing, windows, doors or structure. Requirements differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, and we help you with the permit requirements for your project.

### Which siding material is best for the Seattle area?

There is no single best choice. Fiber cement is popular for its durability. Vinyl costs less and needs little upkeep. Wood has a classic look but needs regular painting or staining. Metal is long lasting. We go through your options with you and tell you honestly which fits your home and budget.

### Can siding be installed in the rain or in winter?

Siding is best installed in dry weather, and the wet season can push a schedule. We plan around the forecast, protect open walls while we work and tell you plainly if weather changes the plan.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your home, look at the siding, and talk about what you have noticed, your goals and your budget. You do not need to know what the problem is. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
