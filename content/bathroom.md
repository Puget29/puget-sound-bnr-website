## Planning Your Bathroom Remodel: Layout and Budget Basics

The decisions you make before construction starts have the biggest effect on how the bathroom feels and what it costs. Here is how we think about them with every homeowner.

### Start with how you use the bathroom

Before choosing tile, think about how the room really works. Who uses it, and at what times of day? Do two people get ready at once? Do you take baths or only showers? Is it a main bathroom, a guest bathroom or a primary suite? The answers decide whether you need a double vanity, a bigger shower, better storage, or just better lighting and ventilation.

### Layout choices

- **Keeping the existing layout** is the most budget friendly path. The toilet, sink and shower stay where the plumbing already is, so the work stays simple.
- **Improving the layout** within the same footprint, for example by swapping a tub for a larger shower or adding a wider vanity, can change how the room feels without moving walls.
- **Reconfiguring the room** by moving the toilet or shower, combining two small spaces or borrowing space from a closet creates the biggest change. It also adds plumbing, structural and permit work, so it should be planned early.

### How a bathroom budget usually breaks down

Every project differs, but as general industry guidance, the money in a bathroom remodel usually goes roughly like this:

| Where the money goes | Typical share of the budget |
| --- | --- |
| Labor and installation | 20% to 35% |
| Tile, flooring and shower surfaces | 15% to 20% |
| Vanity, cabinets and countertop | 10% to 15% |
| Plumbing fixtures and fittings | 10% to 15% |
| Lighting, ventilation, paint and accessories | The remaining share |

These are planning guides, not a quote. Your real numbers depend on your home, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve. Bathrooms are where hidden problems tend to show up, because water finds its way into places nobody can see. Once the old tile and walls come out, we sometimes find soft subfloor, old leaks, rot or outdated plumbing. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Permits

Many bathroom changes need a permit, especially moving plumbing, changing electrical or altering walls. Simple updates, like replacing a vanity, a toilet or fixtures in the same place, often do not. Rules differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, so we check what applies to your project during the planning stage.

## Waterproofing, Ventilation and Where Quality Actually Matters

In a bathroom, the most important work is often the work you never see. Here is how we help homeowners decide where to put the budget.

### Waterproofing

A beautiful shower that leaks is a failed shower. The tile you see is not what keeps water out. The waterproofing behind it does that. A shower needs a properly sloped base, a waterproof membrane or system, and carefully sealed corners, seams and penetrations before any tile goes on. This is not the place to cut corners, because a failure shows up years later behind the wall, and by then the repair is far bigger than the savings.

### Ventilation

The Pacific Northwest is damp for much of the year, and a bathroom without good ventilation holds moisture. That leads to peeling paint, mildew and eventually damage inside the walls. A properly sized exhaust fan that vents outside, and that you actually run during and after showers, protects the whole room. We plan it with the rest of the project, not as an afterthought.

### Shower, tub or both

- **Walk-in shower.** The most popular choice. It is easy to get into, it makes a small bathroom feel larger, and a curbless design is also a good option for accessibility.
- **Tub and shower combination.** A practical choice for a family bathroom where one tub is the only tub in the house.
- **Freestanding or soaking tub.** A real comfort in a primary bathroom, but it needs the floor space and plumbing to support it.

### Tile and surfaces

- **Porcelain and ceramic tile** are durable, water resistant and available in nearly any look. Porcelain is especially tough.
- **Natural stone** is beautiful but needs sealing and more care in wet areas.
- **Large format tile** means fewer grout lines, which makes cleaning easier and the room look more open.
- **Solid surface and engineered panels** can be a practical, low-maintenance option for shower walls.

We will show you samples in your own bathroom light, because tile and color look different there than in a showroom.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on what you cannot easily replace: waterproofing, ventilation, plumbing quality, tile installation and the shower itself. Save on things that are easy to upgrade later, such as decorative lighting, mirrors, towel hardware and paint colors. Our job is to help you put the budget where you will notice it.

## Living Through a Bathroom Renovation

A remodel disrupts daily life for a while, and it is better to know that up front. Here is what to expect and how we make it easier.

- **Timeline.** Planning and design usually take a few weeks. Vanities, glass and special order tile can add lead time. Construction itself typically takes a few weeks for a smaller remodel and longer for a full renovation. We give you a specific schedule in writing once the plan is set.
- **No bathroom for a while.** If this is your only bathroom, plan ahead for where to shower and use the toilet during the work. If you have a second bathroom, you will use that one. We tell you in advance when the water will be off.
- **Dust and noise.** Demolition is the loudest and dirtiest stage. We seal off the work area, protect floors and surfaces, and clean up at the end of every work day.
- **Pets and children.** Keep them away from the work zone. We will tell you when the noisiest days are so you can plan around them.
- **Decisions.** We will ask you to choose tile, fixtures and colors on a schedule, because late changes slow the project and add cost. We help you make each choice in time.
- **Communication.** You will know who is on site, what is happening this week and what comes next. If something unexpected appears behind a wall, we explain it, show you the options and agree on the next step with you before we continue.

A well planned remodel feels calm even when the bathroom is torn apart, and that is what we aim for.

## Bathroom Remodeling FAQs

### How long does a bathroom remodel take?

It depends on the scope. Replacing a vanity, toilet and fixtures takes much less time than a full renovation with a new shower, new tile and moved plumbing. Most of the schedule is made up of design, permits where needed, special order items, and construction after that. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does a bathroom remodel cost?

The cost depends on the size of the bathroom, the layout, the tile and fixtures you choose, and how much plumbing and electrical work is needed. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your bathroom and give you a clear written estimate.

### Do I need a permit to remodel my bathroom?

Often yes, especially when the work involves moving plumbing, changing electrical or altering walls. Replacing a vanity or fixtures in the same locations may not need one. Requirements differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, and we help you with the permit requirements for your project.

### Can I live in my home during the remodel?

Most homeowners do. If it is your only bathroom, you will need a plan for part of the project, and we help you work that out before we start. We contain dust, protect your floors and clean up at the end of each day.

### Can you help me design the layout?

Yes. Planning and design are part of the process. We look at how you use the bathroom, suggest layout options and help you choose tile, fixtures, a vanity and lighting that work together.

### Can you turn my tub into a walk-in shower?

Often, yes. It is one of the most common bathroom updates. It involves removing the tub, rebuilding the floor and drain, and waterproofing the new shower properly. We look at the space during the consultation and tell you what is possible and what it involves.

### Why does waterproofing matter so much?

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. The waterproofing layer behind and under them is what keeps water out of the walls and floor. When it is done well, the shower lasts for many years. When it is skipped or rushed, leaks stay hidden until the damage is large and expensive to repair.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your home, look at the bathroom, and talk about your goals, your wish list and your budget. You do not need a finished design. Bring photos or ideas if you have them. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
