## Planning Your ADU or Garage Conversion: Options and Budget Basics

The decisions you make before construction starts have the biggest effect on how the unit works and what it costs. Here is how we think about them with every homeowner.

### Start with who the space is for

Before choosing finishes, decide who will live there and for how long. A parent who needs easy access has different needs than a young adult, a guest or a tenant. The answers decide the layout, how private the unit needs to be, how many bedrooms make sense and what kind of kitchen and bathroom it needs.

### Options to consider

- **Garage conversion.** Often the most direct path, because the walls and roof already exist. It involves the floor, insulation, windows, utilities and a plan for where your cars will go.
- **Interior conversion.** A basement or part of the house becomes a separate unit. It usually needs its own entrance, proper exits and attention to ceiling height, moisture and sound.
- **Attached or detached units.** Depending on your property and the local rules, it may be possible to add a new unit to the house or in the yard. These involve the most design, structure and permit work, and we tell you honestly what fits your lot.

### How an ADU budget usually breaks down

Every project differs, but as general industry guidance, the money in an ADU or garage conversion usually goes roughly like this:

| Where the money goes | Typical share of the budget |
| --- | --- |
| Kitchen and bathroom (plumbing, cabinets, fixtures, tile) | 25% to 35% |
| Structure, framing, insulation, windows and doors | 20% to 30% |
| Electrical, heating and ventilation | 10% to 20% |
| Design, permits and fees | 5% to 10% |
| Flooring, paint and finishes | The remaining share |

These are planning guides, not a quote. Your real numbers depend on your property, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve. Garages and older spaces often hide things nobody could see before, such as uneven slabs, moisture, outdated wiring or utilities that need upgrading. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Permits and local rules

This is the part of an ADU project that most often surprises homeowners. Rules for accessory dwelling units and garage conversions differ between cities and can change over time. They can cover lot size, setbacks, height, parking, utility connections and who may live on the property. A garage conversion and a new living space almost always need permits. We check the current rules for your property during the planning stage, and we help you with the permit requirements for your project. Rules about renting out a unit are separate, so check them for your city before you plan around rental income.

## Turning a Garage Into a Home: Where Quality Actually Matters

A garage is built to hold cars, not people. Making it a comfortable, safe place to live takes more than drywall and paint. Here is what we plan for.

### The floor and moisture

Garage slabs are often lower than the rest of the house, can slope toward a drain and may let moisture through. A good conversion deals with the floor first, with proper moisture control, leveling and an insulated floor system where it makes sense, so the room is dry, flat and warm underfoot.

### Insulation and comfort

Garage walls and ceilings are often uninsulated. Proper insulation, air sealing and quality windows are what make the unit comfortable in a cold, wet winter and a warm summer, and they keep energy bills down.

### Heating and ventilation

A living space needs a reliable heat source and fresh air. We plan heating and ventilation as part of the design, including bathroom and kitchen exhaust, so moisture does not build up inside.

### Light, exits and safety

Living spaces need natural light, safe ways out and working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Bedrooms need proper exit windows, and the unit needs a safe separation from the rest of the home where the building code calls for it.

### Power and plumbing

New living space often needs more electrical capacity than a garage has, and water and sewer connections for the bathroom and kitchen. We look at your panel and your utility connections early, because they can affect cost and schedule.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on what you cannot easily redo: structure, moisture control, insulation, windows, plumbing, electrical and good installation. Save on finishes that are easy to upgrade later, such as light fixtures, paint and hardware. Our job is to help you put the budget where the unit will perform best.

## ADU and Garage Conversion FAQs

### What is an ADU?

An accessory dwelling unit is a separate, self contained living space on the same property as a main home. It has its own entrance, a bathroom, and usually a kitchen or kitchenette. It can be built inside the house, attached to it, converted from a garage, or placed as a separate building, depending on the property and the local rules.

### Can I turn my garage into a living space?

Often yes, but it depends on your city, your lot and the garage itself. Rules cover things like parking, utilities and the size and use of the unit. We look at your garage and your property during the free consultation and tell you what is possible.

### How long does a garage conversion take?

It depends on the scope. Planning, design and permits usually take the longest, and construction takes a few weeks to a few months after that. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does an ADU or garage conversion cost?

The cost depends on the size, the condition of the space, the utilities, the kitchen and bathroom you choose, and the finishes. A garage conversion usually costs less than a new detached unit, because the walls and roof already exist. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your property and give you a clear written estimate.

### Do I need a permit?

Almost always yes for a new living space. Requirements differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, and they can change over time. We help you with the permit requirements for your project and check the current rules for your property.

### Can I rent out the unit?

Many homeowners do, but the rules about renting an ADU, and about short term rentals, differ by city and can change. We build the unit to be a safe, comfortable home, and we suggest checking your city's current rental rules before you plan around rental income.

### Where will I park if the garage becomes a living space?

That depends on your property and the local rules, and we look at it early in the planning stage. Parking requirements differ by city, and the layout of the driveway and lot affect your options.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your property, look at the garage or space, and talk about your goals, your wish list and your budget. You do not need a finished design. Bring photos or ideas if you have them. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
