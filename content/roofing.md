## Planning Your Roof Project: Repair, Replace and Budget Basics

The decision between repairing and replacing a roof has the biggest effect on what you spend. Here is how we think about it with every homeowner.

### Signs your roof needs attention

- Water stains on ceilings or walls, especially after heavy rain
- Missing, cracked, curled or loose shingles
- Granules from the shingles collecting in gutters or at the downspouts
- Daylight visible in the attic, or damp, dark patches on the underside of the roof deck
- Heavy moss growth, or sagging areas on the roof
- A roof that is simply old, even if there are no obvious problems yet

### Repair or replace

- **A repair** makes sense when the damage is limited and the rest of the roof is in good shape. A few damaged shingles, a failed flashing or a single leak can often be fixed without replacing everything.
- **A replacement** makes sense when the roof is near the end of its life, when problems keep coming back, or when damage is widespread. Paying for repeated repairs on a roof that is worn out usually costs more than replacing it.
- **We will tell you honestly which one you need.** Sometimes that means a smaller job than you expected, and that is fine with us.

### Roofing materials and how long they typically last

Every roof differs, and lifespan depends on installation, ventilation and upkeep. As general industry guidance, typical ranges look like this:

| Material | Typical lifespan |
| --- | --- |
| Asphalt shingles | About 15 to 30 years |
| Metal roofing | About 40 to 70 years |
| Cedar shake | About 20 to 40 years |
| Synthetic and composite roofing | About 30 to 50 years |

These are planning guides, not a promise about your roof. Your real numbers depend on your home, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve for a replacement. Once old roofing comes off, we sometimes find soft or rotted decking, old leaks or damaged framing that nobody could see from above. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Permits

Many cities require a permit for a full roof replacement, and some repairs need one too, especially when the structure is involved. Rules differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, so we check what applies to your project during the planning stage.

## Roofing in the Pacific Northwest: What Is Different Here

Roofing in a dry climate is not the same as roofing in Puget Sound. Here is what we plan for.

### Moss and constant moisture

Moss holds water against the roof surface, lifts shingle edges and can shorten the life of a roof if it is left alone. Shaded roofs under trees collect it fastest. Keeping the roof clean and the gutters flowing is part of making a roof last in this climate.

### Gutters and drainage

Needles and leaves clog gutters quickly in many Seattle area neighborhoods. When gutters back up, water can get behind the edge of the roof and into the fascia and walls. We look at how the roof drains as part of every inspection.

### Ventilation and the attic

A roof is a system, not just a surface. Without enough attic ventilation, moisture builds up in the damp months and can cause mold, rot and damaged insulation. We check the airflow and plan it into a replacement.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on what you cannot see and cannot easily redo: the decking, underlayment, flashing and good installation. A roof is only as good as its weakest detail. Save on things that do not change how the roof performs. Our job is to help you put the budget where it protects your home the most.

## Living Through a Roofing Project

A roofing project is noisy for a few days, and it is better to know that up front. Here is what to expect and how we make it easier.

- **Timeline.** Many residential roofs take a few days in good weather, and larger or more complex roofs take longer. Weather in the Puget Sound area can move the schedule, and we tell you plainly if it does. We give you a specific schedule in writing once the plan is set.
- **Noise and vibration.** Tear-off and installation are loud, and the house can shake a little. Plan for a noisy few days, and keep fragile items off shelves and walls, especially on the upper floor.
- **Attic and ceilings.** Cover or move stored items in the attic, since dust and debris can fall through gaps in the decking.
- **Driveway and yard.** We need space for a dumpster and materials. Park off the driveway and let us know about gardens, patios or fragile plants we should protect.
- **Pets and children.** Keep them away from the work zone and from the yard under the roof. We will tell you when the noisiest days are so you can plan around them.
- **Communication.** You will know who is on site, what is happening this week and what comes next. If something unexpected appears under the old roofing, we explain it, show you the options and agree on the next step with you before we continue.

A well planned roofing project feels organized even on the loudest day, and that is what we aim for.

## Roofing FAQs

### How long does a roof replacement take?

It depends on the size of the roof, its shape and the weather. Many residential roofs take a few days in good conditions, and larger or more complex roofs take longer. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does a roof repair or replacement cost?

The cost depends on the size and pitch of the roof, the material you choose, the condition of the decking underneath and how complex the roof is. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your roof and give you a clear written estimate.

### Should I repair or replace my roof?

If the damage is limited and the rest of the roof is in good shape, a repair is often enough. If the roof is near the end of its life or problems keep returning, replacement usually makes more sense. We will tell you honestly which one fits your roof.

### How do I know if my roof has a leak?

Common signs are stains on ceilings or walls, peeling paint, damp insulation or a musty smell in the attic. Water often travels before it shows up, so the stain can be far from the actual leak. A roof inspection finds the source.

### Do I need a permit to replace my roof?

Often yes for a full replacement, and sometimes for repairs that involve the structure. Requirements differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, and we help you with the permit requirements for your project.

### Can a roof be installed in the rain or in winter?

Roofing is best done in dry weather, and the wet season can push a schedule. We plan around the forecast, protect the open roof while we work and tell you plainly if weather changes the plan.

### Does moss damage my roof?

It can. Moss holds moisture against the roof, can lift shingle edges and speeds up wear, especially on shaded roofs. We assess it during the inspection and tell you whether it is a cosmetic issue or a real problem.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your home, look at the roof, and talk about what you have noticed, your goals and your budget. You do not need to know what the problem is. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
