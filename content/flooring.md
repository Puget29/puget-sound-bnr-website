## Planning Your Flooring Project: Materials and Budget Basics

The decisions you make before installation starts have the biggest effect on how the floor looks and how long it lasts. Here is how we think about them with every homeowner.

### Start with how you use each room

Before choosing a look, think about how each room really works. Are there kids and pets? Does the room get wet? Is it a high traffic hallway or a quiet bedroom? Is there a basement or a concrete slab underneath? The answers decide which materials make sense, and often different rooms call for different floors.

### Flooring materials and how long they typically last

Every home differs, and lifespan depends on the product, the installation and how the floor is used and cared for. As general industry guidance, typical ranges look like this:

| Material | Typical lifespan |
| --- | --- |
| Solid hardwood | 30 years or more, and can be refinished |
| Engineered wood | About 20 to 40 years |
| Tile | About 20 to 50 years or more |
| Luxury vinyl plank | About 10 to 25 years |
| Laminate | About 10 to 20 years |
| Carpet | About 8 to 15 years |

These are planning guides, not a promise about your floors. Your real numbers depend on your home, your choices and the scope of work, and we put them in writing after the consultation.

### How a flooring budget usually breaks down

As general industry guidance, the money in a flooring project usually goes roughly like this:

| Where the money goes | Typical share of the budget |
| --- | --- |
| Flooring materials | 35% to 50% |
| Labor and installation | 25% to 35% |
| Removal, subfloor repair and preparation | 10% to 20% |
| Trim, transitions and finishing | The remaining share |

These are planning guides, not a quote.

### Keep a contingency

We recommend keeping 10% to 15% of the budget in reserve. Once old flooring comes up, we sometimes find uneven or damaged subfloor, old water damage or moisture problems that nobody could see before. A reserve turns a surprise into a manageable decision instead of a crisis.

### Permits

Flooring on its own usually does not need a permit. Projects that also involve structural repairs, plumbing or other changes might. Rules differ between Seattle, Bellevue and other cities, so we check what applies to your project during the planning stage.

## Preparation, Moisture and Where Quality Actually Matters

The floor you see is only part of the story. What is underneath decides how it looks and how long it lasts. Here is how we help homeowners decide where to put the budget.

### The subfloor

A new floor will copy whatever is beneath it. Dips, high spots, loose boards and damage under the old floor show up on top of the new one as gaps, squeaks and uneven spots. We check the subfloor, repair and level it where needed, and prepare it properly before anything goes down.

### Moisture

The Pacific Northwest is damp for much of the year, and moisture is the biggest enemy of many floors, especially over concrete slabs and above crawl spaces. We look at moisture conditions before choosing a material, and we use the right underlayment and moisture control where the situation calls for it.

### Choosing the right material for each room

- **Hardwood and engineered wood** bring warmth and last for decades, but they do not like standing water. They suit living rooms, bedrooms and hallways.
- **Luxury vinyl plank** handles water, pets and heavy use well, which makes it a popular choice for kitchens, basements and family rooms.
- **Tile** is durable and handles water, so it is a natural fit for bathrooms, entries and kitchens.
- **Laminate** is an affordable option with a wide choice of looks, but it is less forgiving of water.
- **Carpet** is soft and warm, and it works well in bedrooms and on stairs.

We will show you samples in your own home light, because color and finish look different there than in a showroom.

### Installation details

Straight lines, tight seams, correct expansion gaps, clean transitions and neat trim are what make a floor look professional. Cheap installation of a good product makes a floor look cheap. We spend our time on these details.

### Where we suggest spending, and where to save

Spend on what you cannot easily redo: subfloor preparation, moisture control, the underlayment and good installation. Save on things that are easy to change later, such as area rugs, trim paint and furniture. Our job is to help you put the budget where you will notice it for years.

## Flooring FAQs

### How long does a flooring replacement take?

It depends on the size of the area, the material and how much preparation the subfloor needs. Many projects take from a few days to a week or two. After your consultation we give you a written schedule for your specific project.

### How much does flooring replacement cost?

The cost depends on the square footage, the material you choose, how much subfloor preparation is needed and how many stairs and transitions there are. The fastest way to get a real number is a free consultation, where we look at your home and give you a clear written estimate.

### Which flooring is best for my home?

It depends on the room, your household and your budget. Hardwood and engineered wood are popular for living areas. Luxury vinyl plank handles water and heavy use well. Tile is a strong choice for wet areas. We go through your options with you and tell you honestly which fits each room.

### Can I refinish my hardwood instead of replacing it?

Solid hardwood can often be sanded and refinished instead of replaced, depending on its thickness and condition. We look at your floors and tell you honestly which makes sense.

### Do I need to move my furniture?

Rooms need to be cleared before the work starts. We talk through the plan with you in advance, so you know what to move and when.

### Can new flooring go over my existing floor?

Sometimes, depending on the material and the condition of the floor underneath. Often it is better to remove the old floor, so the subfloor can be checked and prepared. We tell you what makes sense for your home.

### What flooring works best over concrete or in a basement?

Moisture is the main issue. Luxury vinyl plank, tile and some engineered products handle these spaces better than solid wood. We check the conditions and recommend what suits your basement or slab.

### What happens at the free consultation?

We visit your home, look at the rooms and the existing floors, and talk about your goals, your wish list and your budget. You do not need a finished design. Bring photos or ideas if you have them. You will leave with a clear idea of the options, and we follow up with a written estimate.
