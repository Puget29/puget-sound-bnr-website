The SOP promised **four substantive SEO content sections** per page. All 81 pages contain them, in the same order, from the same template. Word counts below are the full section (heading + lede + body).

| Section (H2) | Source | Templated? | Words (min/med/max) | Carries local substance? |

| `Kitchen and Bathroom Remodeling for <City> Homes` | hardcoded template | **100% templated** | 116 / 116 / 124 | No — only the city token |
| `What <City> Homeowners Should Know Before a Home Renovation` | `local_bullets[4]` | **~50% templated** | 128 / 137 / 147 | Partly — bullet 3 is usually city-specific; bullets 1,2,4 are boilerplate |
| `Remodeling Homes in <City>` | `local_body` | **unique** | 36 / 43 / 77 | **Yes — the only genuinely unique prose**, but the thinnest section |

Supporting (non-SEO) sections, all 100% templated across the 81 pages: *Why <City> Homeowners Choose Puget Sound BNR* (124w), *What We Build and Renovate* (services carousel), *What clients say* (shared review deck), *Check our last jobs* (shared gallery), *A Clear Remodeling Process...* (124-138w), *Serving <City> and Nearby Communities*, *Around <City> and How We Get There* (POIs).

## Finding

Of the four 'substantive SEO sections', **two are entirely generic** (Planning, Kitchen & Bath), **one is half-generic** (Before a Renovation), and **only one is genuinely unique** (Remodeling Homes) — and that one is the shortest on the page (median 43 words, as low as 32). The *structure* of substantive content exists; the *substance* that differentiates one city from another is roughly **130-150 words per page** out of ~1,640.
